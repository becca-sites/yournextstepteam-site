## Which streets flood, actually

The Snoqualmie River runs the length of the valley, and in any given winter a few neighborhoods see water in places they would rather not. The official FEMA flood map is where the conversation starts. Some streets that look safe on paper have drainage history that locals know about and disclosures don't always catch. A 30-minute call with anyone who has lived in town for a decade will sort the rumor from the reality.

## Which schools actually walk to town

Cherry Valley Elementary is the walk-zone school for a chunk of central Duvall. Outside that radius, kids are on a bus route or in a parent carpool. If walk-zone matters to you, the difference can be three blocks, so it pays to check before you write an offer.

## What the Seattle commute actually feels like in February

Forty-five minutes is a fair-weather number. In summer, with light traffic and no rain on the road, that is what you will see. In a wet Tuesday morning in February with a chain-reaction slowdown on the 522, you will see 70 to 80 minutes. The valley buyers who love it here all made peace with that range up front. The buyers who regret moving out were sold on the 45-minute number and not the range.

## Why people still move here anyway

Because the trade-off works. Bigger lots, a school district that punches above its weight, a downtown where people know each other, the Snoqualmie Valley Trail running through town, and an hour to the mountains when the snow is good. None of that shows up in a listing photo.
