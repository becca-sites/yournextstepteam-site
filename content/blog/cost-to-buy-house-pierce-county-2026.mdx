# How Much Does It Cost to Buy a House in Pierce County in 2026?

The median home price in Pierce County is sitting around $555,000 to $575,000 in 2026, but your actual total cost to buy will land somewhere between 103% and 106% of the purchase price once you factor in closing costs, inspections, and the other line items that catch buyers off guard. The purchase price is just the starting number, not the finish line.

I work with buyers across Pierce County every week, and the number one question I hear in that first phone call is some version of "what am I actually looking at here?" So let's break down the real numbers, area by area, with no sugarcoating.

{/* PHOTO: Neighborhood street scene in a Pierce County community, sidewalks and landscaping visible, no people */}

## What Are Home Prices Like Across Pierce County Right Now?

Pierce County is not one market. It's a collection of very different communities, and the price tags reflect that. Here's where things stand in early 2026:

**Bonney Lake** is currently the highest-priced area I work in regularly, with median prices around $720,000. Bonney Lake has seen strong demand thanks to newer construction, larger lots, and proximity to both Sumner and the Plateau area. If you're looking for a newer home with square footage, this is where you'll find it, but you'll pay for it.

**North Tacoma** runs around $650,000 to $675,000 for the median. North Tacoma brings character homes, walkability, and some of the best views in the county. The trade-off is that you're often buying a home built in the 1920s through 1950s, which means more character but also more maintenance conversations during inspection.

**Puyallup** sits around $580,000 at the median. Puyallup offers a solid middle ground with a mix of established neighborhoods and newer developments. It's one of the areas I recommend most often to buyers who want good value without sacrificing access to amenities.

**Eatonville** comes in around $585,000, which surprises a lot of people. Eatonville used to be the "affordable option," and while it's still less expensive than Bonney Lake, prices have climbed. What you get in Eatonville is land, privacy, and proximity to Mount Rainier. If you're someone who wants acreage and breathing room, it's worth a serious look.

## How Much Do I Need for a Down Payment in Pierce County?

This is where I see the most confusion, because a lot of people still believe you need 20% down to buy a home. You don't.

Here's what down payments actually look like in 2026:

**Conventional loans** require as little as 3% down for first-time buyers and 5% for repeat buyers. On a $575,000 home, that's $17,250 to $28,750. You will pay private mortgage insurance (PMI) if you put down less than 20%, but PMI is not the dealbreaker people think it is. It typically runs $100 to $250 per month and drops off once you hit 20% equity.

**FHA loans** require 3.5% down. On that same $575,000 home, you're looking at about $20,125.

**VA loans** require zero down if you're an eligible veteran or active-duty service member. I've closed several VA purchases in Pierce County (proximity to Joint Base Lewis-McChord means I work with military buyers often), and the zero-down benefit is genuinely life-changing for buyers who qualify.

**USDA loans** also offer zero down for eligible rural areas. Parts of Pierce County, including areas around Eatonville and some unincorporated zones, may qualify. It's worth checking.

## What About Closing Costs?

Buyer closing costs in Washington State typically run 2% to 5% of the purchase price. On a $575,000 home, that's roughly $11,500 to $28,750.

Here's what's included in that number:

- **Lender fees:** origination charges, underwriting, credit report, flood certification
- **Title and escrow fees:** title insurance, escrow fees, recording fees
- **Prepaid items:** homeowner's insurance premium, property taxes (prorated), prepaid interest
- **Inspection costs:** home inspection ($400 to $600), sewer scope ($150 to $250), septic inspection if applicable ($300 to $500)

One thing I always tell my buyers: closing costs are negotiable. In some market conditions, I can negotiate for the seller to contribute toward your closing costs. It depends on the market, the property, and the seller's motivation, but it's always worth discussing as part of your offer strategy.

## What Expenses Do Most Buyers Forget About?

Honestly, this is the section I wish someone had handed me a list for when I bought my first home. The purchase price and closing costs are the obvious numbers. These are the ones that sneak up on you:

**Earnest money** is due within a few days of mutual acceptance, typically 1% to 3% of the purchase price. It gets applied to your down payment at closing, but you need it in liquid funds right away. On a $575,000 home, that's $5,750 to $17,250 within days of your offer being accepted.

**Home inspection** runs $400 to $600 for a standard inspection. I strongly recommend adding a sewer scope and septic inspection if applicable. Budget $700 to $1,200 total.

**Appraisal fees** run $500 to $800, required by your lender. Not refundable if the deal falls through.

**Moving costs** run $1,500 to $4,000 for a local Pierce County move. (No judgment on how much stuff you've accumulated. I've seen it all.)

**Immediate home expenses** sneak up on every new homeowner. New locks, a lawn mower, basic tools, that light fixture that was "fine during the showing" but drives you crazy once you live there. Budget $1,000 to $3,000 for first-month surprises.

## Are There Programs That Help With These Costs?

Yes, and I think they're underused. Washington State has several down payment assistance programs that Pierce County buyers can access:

The **Washington State Housing Finance Commission (WSHFC)** offers down payment assistance paired with 30-year fixed-rate mortgages. Their programs work with FHA, VA, USDA, and conventional loans.

The **HomeChoice program** provides up to $15,000 as a deferred second mortgage with 1% interest and no monthly payments until you sell or refinance. This one's specifically designed for first-time buyers who need help bridging the gap.

The **Covenant Homeownership Program** offers zero-interest down payment and closing cost assistance as a secondary loan, repaid when you sell or refinance.

If you're buying within **Tacoma city limits**, the City of Tacoma's down payment assistance program offers up to $60,000 as a no-interest, 25-year loan for first-time buyers. That's a significant amount of help.

I always recommend that my buyers talk to a lender who's familiar with these programs early in the process. Not every lender knows how to work with them, so finding the right lender matters.

## So What's the Real Bottom Line?

For a median-priced Pierce County home around $575,000, here's a realistic budget:

- **Down payment (5%):** $28,750
- **Closing costs (3%):** $17,250
- **Inspections:** $800
- **Appraisal:** $600
- **Earnest money (applied to down payment):** $5,750
- **Moving and first-month expenses:** $3,000

**Total cash needed at or before closing: roughly $50,400.** And that's with only 5% down. Put down 3%, and you're closer to $39,000.

These numbers aren't meant to scare you. They're meant to prepare you. I'd rather you walk into this process knowing exactly what to expect than get blindsided by a line item on your closing disclosure.
