# Farmers Markets Near Bonney Lake and Puyallup: A Local Guide

One of the things I love most about living in Pierce County is that "eating local" isn't just a bumper sticker here. It's a Wednesday evening at Allan Yorke Park with live music and fresh strawberries. It's a Saturday morning in Puyallup surrounded by 150 vendors and the smell of wood-fired pizza. It's a thing people actually do, regularly, and it's one of the first things I tell people about when they're moving to the area.

So here's your guide to the farmers markets near Bonney Lake and Puyallup, plus a few others worth the drive.

## What Farmers Markets Are Near Bonney Lake?

### Bonney Lake Farmers Market

**When:** Wednesday evenings, June through August
**Where:** Allan Yorke Park, Bonney Lake

This one's special because it lines up with the Tunes at Tapps concert series. So you get live music, local vendors, and a sunset over Lake Tapps all in the same evening. It's smaller than the Puyallup market, but that's part of the charm. You'll find local produce, baked goods, handmade crafts, and usually some kind of prepared food that smells too good to walk past.

I think the Wednesday evening timing is genius. It turns a midweek errand into an actual outing. Grab dinner from a vendor, listen to the music, let the kids run around the park. It's the kind of thing that makes you feel like you live in a small town, even though you're 30 minutes from Tacoma.

### Orting Valley Farmers Market

**When:** Fridays, 3:00 PM to 7:00 PM, May 15 through August 28
**Where:** North Park, 101 Washington Ave N, Orting

Orting is about a 20-minute drive from Bonney Lake, and the Friday afternoon market there is worth it. It's got that small-town, everybody-knows-everybody energy. The produce is hyper-local (we're talking farms you can see from the road on your drive in), and the vendors are genuinely excited to tell you about what they're growing.

The Friday afternoon timing also means you can swing by after work and grab everything you need for the weekend. I've done this more times than I can count.

## What About the Puyallup Farmers Market?

### Puyallup Farmers Market

**When:** Saturdays, 9:00 AM to 2:00 PM, April 18 through October 10 (2026 season)
**Where:** Pioneer Park and Pavilion, Puyallup
**Note:** Closed June 13 for Meeker Days

This is the big one. Up to 150 vendors, indoor and outdoor, and one of the largest farmers markets in Washington state. It's not just produce; you'll find baked goods, specialty foods, artisan crafts, prepared meals, flowers, honey, jams, sauces, and usually at least one booth that makes you say "I didn't know I needed that, but now I absolutely do."

The indoor/outdoor setup means it runs rain or shine, which matters a lot in the Pacific Northwest. April can be unpredictable, but the pavilion keeps the core market dry.

A few things to know before you go:

- **Arrive early.** The best produce goes fast, especially berries in season. By 11:00 AM the blueberry situation can get dire.
- **Bring cash.** Many vendors take cards now, but some of the smaller booths are cash only.
- **Bring your own bags.** Some vendors have them, but you'll be happier with your own, especially if you're buying a lot.
- **The market is closed June 13 for Meeker Days.** Meeker Days is Puyallup's big annual festival, so the park is otherwise occupied that weekend.

Honestly, the Puyallup Farmers Market is one of the top things I recommend to anyone moving to the area. It's a Saturday morning tradition for a lot of people around here, and it's a genuinely fun way to spend a few hours.

## Are There Year-Round Farmers Markets Nearby?

### Proctor Farmers Market (Tacoma)

**When:** Saturdays, 9:00 AM to 2:00 PM, April 4 through December 19
**Where:** N 27th and Proctor St, Tacoma

Yes. The Proctor Farmers Market in Tacoma's Proctor District runs from early April through mid-December, which is about as close to year-round as you'll find in the Pacific Northwest. It's a 25 to 30 minute drive from Bonney Lake, and it's absolutely worth it.

The Proctor District itself is a great neighborhood to walk around. Cute shops, good coffee, and the market fits right into that vibe. The vendor mix leans a little more artisan and specialty food compared to Puyallup, but you'll still find plenty of fresh produce, especially during peak season.

I think Proctor is the best market to visit if you want the full "Saturday morning at the market" experience. Grab a pastry, browse the booths, get your vegetables for the week, and then walk down Proctor St for lunch. That's a solid morning.

## What About Gig Harbor?

### Gig Harbor Waterfront Farmers Market

**When:** Thursdays, 1:00 PM to 6:00 PM, May 21 through August 27
**Where:** Skansie Brothers Park, Gig Harbor

If you're on the Gig Harbor side of the county (or willing to make the drive across the Narrows Bridge), this Thursday afternoon market is a treat. Skansie Brothers Park is right on the waterfront, so you're shopping for produce with a view of the harbor. The setting alone makes it worth the trip.

The vendor selection is solid: fresh seafood, produce, baked goods, flowers, and crafts. It's smaller than Puyallup but bigger than Orting, and the Thursday timing means it doesn't compete with any of the Saturday markets.

## What Should I Expect at a Pierce County Farmers Market?

If you haven't been to a farmers market in Pierce County before, here's the general vibe:

**Produce:** Seasonal fruits and vegetables, often picked that morning or the day before. Strawberries and cherries in June, blueberries and peaches in July and August, apples and squash in the fall. Washington state grows some of the best produce in the country, and the stuff at these markets is noticeably better than what you'll find at the grocery store.

**Baked goods:** Bread, pastries, cookies, pies. Usually from local bakeries or home bakers. The sourdough game at these markets is strong.

**Prepared foods:** Wood-fired pizza, tamales, kettle corn, crepes, BBQ. Plan to eat lunch at the market. You won't regret it.

**Crafts and artisan goods:** Handmade soap, candles, jewelry, pottery, woodwork. Great for gifts (or for treating yourself).

**Live music:** Several of these markets feature live music, especially the Bonney Lake market during Tunes at Tapps and the Puyallup market on select Saturdays.

**The people:** Farmers market regulars are friendly. Vendors will tell you how to cook something you've never heard of. The person next to you in line will have a recommendation. It's a community thing, and it feels like one.

## What Are Becca's Tips for Getting the Most Out of Market Season?

Okay, here's what I've learned from many, many Saturday mornings:

1. **Do a lap first.** Walk the whole market before you buy anything. You'll see what's available, compare prices, and avoid the "I already bought tomatoes but those tomatoes are better" problem.

2. **Arrive early for produce, later for deals.** The best selection is in the first hour. But if you show up in the last 30 minutes, some vendors will cut prices rather than pack everything back up.

3. **Talk to the vendors.** Ask what's good this week. Ask how to cook it. They love talking about their stuff, and you'll learn things you won't find on Google.

4. **Bring a cooler if it's hot.** Especially for meat, dairy, or anything perishable. Your car gets warm fast in July.

5. **Check the schedules.** Markets have different days, different hours, and some close for local events (like Puyallup closing for Meeker Days). A quick check before you head out saves a wasted trip.

These markets exist because there are local farms producing food, local makers creating goods, and local people showing up every week to support them.
