Buying a home with cash sounds like it should be the easiest transaction in real estate. No lender. No underwriting. No nail-biting over whether the appraisal will come back low enough to tank the deal.

And honestly? Cash purchases *are* simpler in a lot of ways. But "simpler" doesn't mean "simple." Especially when your buyers have a very specific list of home features they absolutely need, and the housing stock in their target area isn't exactly overflowing with options.

This is the story of a cash purchase I handled in the Tacoma area for a couple who knew exactly what they needed in a home. Not what they wanted. What they *needed*. There's a big difference, and it changed everything about how I approached this search.

## What Does a Cash Home Purchase Actually Look Like in Tacoma?

So let's start with the basics, because I think there's a lot of confusion about how cash purchases work in real estate. People hear "cash buyer" and picture someone showing up with a briefcase full of hundred-dollar bills. (I've never seen that happen, for the record. But if it ever does, I'm absolutely bringing snacks to that closing.)

A cash purchase means the buyer doesn't need a mortgage loan to buy the property. They're paying the full purchase price with their own funds. In practical terms, here's what that changes:

**No lender involvement.** This is the big one. No loan application, no underwriting process, no mortgage contingency. The buyer provides proof of funds (a bank statement or letter from their financial institution showing they have the money) and that's it. No waiting six weeks for a loan officer to get back from vacation.

**Faster closing timeline.** A financed purchase in Pierce County typically takes 30 to 45 days to close. Cash deals? I've closed them in as few as 10 days. This particular deal closed in about three weeks because I wanted time for inspections and due diligence without rushing.

**Fewer contingencies.** Cash offers can (but don't always) waive the financing contingency, which makes the offer stronger in competitive situations. You're telling the seller, "This deal isn't going to fall apart because my lender got cold feet." That's a powerful message in a market where sellers have been burned by financing fall-throughs.

**No appraisal requirement.** Since there's no lender, there's no mandatory appraisal. You *can* still get one if you want to make sure you're not overpaying, and I usually recommend it. But it's your call, not the bank's.

For this particular deal in the Tacoma area, the cash position gave my buyers a serious competitive advantage. But it also meant I needed to be extra sharp on other aspects of the transaction, because moving fast without a lender safety net means your agent better be doing their homework.

## Why Would Cash Buyers Still Need a Real Estate Agent?

I get this question a lot, and I totally understand why. If you're not dealing with a lender, why do you need an agent? Can't you just find a house, make an offer, and close?

Technically, yes. Practically? Please don't.

Here's why cash buyers absolutely still need an agent, and honestly, this deal is a perfect example:

**Market knowledge matters more, not less.** Without an appraisal backstop, you're relying on your agent's pricing expertise to make sure you're paying a fair price. I ran comps on every property my buyers considered, because overpaying with cash is still overpaying. There's no lender flagging it for you.

**Negotiation positioning.** Cash is only a competitive advantage if you know how to position it. I don't just write "cash offer" on the purchase agreement and hope the listing agent is impressed. I call the listing agent, explain the strength of the offer, provide proof of funds upfront, and frame the deal in a way that makes the seller feel confident this is the surest path to closing. That's agent work.

**Contract protection.** Washington state real estate contracts are complex. Inspection contingencies, title review, earnest money timelines, possession dates. All of that still applies whether you're paying cash or financing. I've seen cash buyers without agents waive things they shouldn't have waived because they didn't understand what they were signing.

**Finding the right property.** This is where it really mattered for this particular deal. My buyers had specific, non-negotiable home feature requirements. They needed me to filter the entire Tacoma-area inventory through a very precise lens, and I needed to know enough about construction and home design to evaluate properties before I ever set up a showing.

## What Specific Home Features Were on the Must-Have List?

I want to be clear about something: when I say my buyers had specific requirements, I mean the physical features of the home itself. This wasn't a wish list. This was a list of structural and design elements that the home absolutely had to have for it to work for their daily life.

Here's what I was looking for:

- **Single-level living.** No stairs to access the main living areas, bedrooms, or bathrooms. This eliminated a huge portion of Tacoma's housing stock right out of the gate, because Pierce County has a lot of split-levels and multi-story homes.
- **Wider doorways and hallways.** Standard interior doorways are about 28 to 30 inches. The requirement was 32 inches minimum, and 36 inches was ideal.
- **A bathroom on the main level with a walk-in or roll-in shower.** No tub-only bathrooms. No step-over shower curbs.
- **An accessible entryway.** Either a no-step entry or a ramp, or at minimum, the ability to add one without major structural changes.
- **Open floor plan.** Fewer walls and tight corners, more open sightlines and room to move.

{/* PHOTO: Close-up of a wide doorway or accessible bathroom feature in a home, showing the architectural detail */}

Now, here's the thing about searching for homes with these features in the Tacoma area: they're not always listed accurately in the MLS. A listing might say "main-floor living" but still have a step up into the bathroom. Or it'll say "open concept" but the hallway to the bedroom is 28 inches wide. I couldn't trust the listing descriptions. I had to verify everything myself, either through listing photos (zooming in on doorframes like a detective, which is for sure not how I pictured my career going) or by calling the listing agent directly before scheduling a showing.

## How Hard Is It to Find Single-Level Homes in Tacoma?

This is a legitimate challenge, and it's worth talking about if you're searching for homes with specific feature requirements in the Tacoma area.

Pierce County's housing stock is diverse, but a lot of it was built in the 1960s through the 1990s, which means split-level designs, sunken living rooms, and multi-story layouts are extremely common. The newer construction in areas like University Place, Lakewood, and parts of South Tacoma tends to have more open floor plans, but it's also priced higher and moves fast.

Here's what I found when I filtered the Tacoma-area inventory for the must-haves:

**The initial search returned a lot of results.** Single-family homes in the Tacoma metro area? Plenty. But once I applied the single-level filter and started checking doorway widths and bathroom configurations, the list got very short, very fast.

**Manufactured homes and ramblers were the sweet spot.** Ranch-style homes (ramblers) from the 1970s and 1980s often had the single-level layout and wider hallways needed. Some manufactured homes in the area also checked the boxes, though lot ownership versus lot lease was a consideration.

**Newer builds with universal design features exist, but they're competitive.** Builders in Pierce County are increasingly incorporating universal design elements (wider doors, curbless showers, lever handles instead of knobs), but these homes attract a lot of buyer interest and tend to sell quickly.

I spent about two weeks doing intensive inventory analysis before I started setting up showings. I wanted to present my buyers with a curated list, not a fire hose of listings they'd have to sort through themselves. By the time I started taking them to homes in person, I'd already eliminated about 80% of the active inventory.

## How Did the Cash Offer Give These Buyers a Competitive Edge?

Okay, so here's where the cash position really paid off.

I found a rambler in the Tacoma area that checked almost every box. Single level, wider-than-standard doorways (34 inches, not quite 36 but workable), a main-level bathroom with a walk-in shower, and a flat entryway from the garage. The backyard was level, the lot was manageable, and the neighborhood was established and quiet.

The problem? There were two other offers on the table.

One was a financed offer at a slightly higher price. The other was a financed offer with an escalation clause. In a lot of situations, the higher offer wins. But here's what I knew from talking with the listing agent: the sellers had already had a deal fall through on a previous listing due to financing issues. They were, understandably, a little gun-shy about going through that again.

So I positioned the cash offer strategically:

1. **Proof of funds submitted with the offer.** Not after acceptance. With. The sellers could see the money was real before they even made a decision.
2. **Clean offer, minimal contingencies.** I kept the inspection contingency (I always recommend this, cash or not) but waived the financing contingency (obviously) and offered a flexible closing timeline.
3. **Personal call to the listing agent.** I called and walked through the offer, emphasized the certainty of a cash close, and highlighted that my buyers were motivated and ready to move quickly.

The sellers accepted the offer over both financed bids. Not because it was the most money, but because the sellers felt confident the deal would actually close. That's the real power of a cash offer in Pierce County real estate; it's not just about the money, it's about the certainty.

## What Happened After Mutual Acceptance?

Once the offer was accepted, things moved quickly. And that's one of the genuine advantages of a cash purchase: the timeline compression.

**Inspections:** I scheduled a general home inspection within four days of mutual acceptance. I also recommended a sewer scope (always do this in Pierce County, especially on older homes) and brought in a specialist to evaluate the bathroom and entryway for potential modifications. Everything came back clean. A few minor items on the inspection report, but nothing structural and nothing that affected the must-have features.

**Title and escrow:** Without a lender in the mix, the title and escrow process was straightforward. No lender title requirements, no mortgage document prep, no last-minute funding wires getting delayed. My transaction coordinator, Allbree Warner, kept everything moving on schedule.

**Closing:** The deal closed in 21 days from mutual acceptance. My buyers had the keys three weeks after I wrote the offer. For comparison, a financed purchase on the same property would have taken 35 to 45 days minimum.

The best part? After closing, my buyers were able to start making the minor modifications they wanted (adding grab bars, adjusting a few door handles, installing a ramp at the back entrance) without any lender restrictions or escrow holdbacks. When you own the home outright from day one, you've got the freedom to make it exactly what you need.

## What Should Cash Buyers in Pierce County Know Before They Start?

If you're a cash buyer looking at homes in Tacoma, Lakewood, University Place, or anywhere in Pierce County, here's what I'd tell you over coffee:

**Get your proof of funds ready before you start looking.** You'll need a recent bank statement or a letter from your financial institution. Having this ready means you can move fast when you find the right home. In a competitive market, speed matters.

**Don't skip the inspection.** I know it's tempting. You're paying cash, you want to close fast, and inspections feel like they slow things down. But a $500 inspection can save you from a $50,000 surprise. I'll always recommend one, and you should always listen. (Okay, you don't *have* to listen to me. But I'll give you a look.)

**Hire an agent who understands cash transactions.** Not every agent has experience with cash deals. The contract structure, the negotiation strategy, and the closing process are all different from a financed purchase. You want someone who's done this before and knows how to position your cash offer effectively.

**Be specific about your non-negotiables.** If you have features that the home absolutely must have, tell your agent upfront and be firm about them. I'd rather spend two extra weeks finding the right home than rush into a property that doesn't actually work for you. Your agent should be willing to do the legwork of verifying features before you ever step foot inside.

**Understand that cash doesn't mean cheap.** You're not getting a discount because you're paying cash. You might get an acceptance advantage in a competitive situation, but the listing price is the listing price. What cash gives you is speed, certainty, and negotiating power. Use it wisely.

## Is Tacoma a Good Market for Cash Buyers Right Now?

Honestly? I think Tacoma is one of the best markets in the Puget Sound region for cash buyers in 2026. Here's why:

**Inventory is stabilizing.** After years of extremely low inventory, Pierce County is seeing more listings come on the market. More options mean more negotiating room, and cash buyers are in a great position to move quickly on the best ones.

**Price points are accessible.** Compared to Seattle and King County, Tacoma and the surrounding Pierce County communities still offer significantly more home for your money. Cash buyers who've been priced out of the Seattle market are finding real value in Tacoma, Lakewood, University Place, and the surrounding areas.

**Sellers love certainty.** In any market, sellers want to know the deal will close. Cash offers provide that certainty, and in a market where some buyers are stretching to qualify for financing, a solid cash offer stands out even more.
