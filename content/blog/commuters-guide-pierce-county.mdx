# The Commuter's Guide: Living in Pierce County, Working in Seattle or Tacoma

Here's the question I hear at least once a week: "I work in Seattle (or Tacoma). Can I actually live in Pierce County and make the commute work?"

Short answer: yes. Thousands of people do it every day.

Longer answer: it depends on where you're coming from, where you're going, how often you go, and whether you're willing to befriend the Sounder train. So let's break it all down.

## What Are the Main Ways to Commute from Pierce County?

You've got four main options, and most people use some combination of them.

### Sounder Train

The Sounder commuter rail is, in my opinion, the best-kept secret for Pierce County commuters heading to Seattle. It runs from Lakewood to Seattle with stops at South Tacoma, Tacoma Dome, Puyallup, Sumner, Auburn, Kent, Tukwila, and Seattle (King Street Station).

Here's what you need to know:

- **Schedule:** Monday through Friday. Morning trains run northbound (toward Seattle), evening trains run southbound (toward Pierce County). This is a commuter train, not an all-day service.
- **Travel time:** About 1 hour from Tacoma Dome to Seattle. Puyallup to Seattle is roughly the same.
- **Fares:** $3.25 to $5.75 depending on distance. That's significantly cheaper than driving when you factor in gas, tolls, and parking.
- **The experience:** You sit down. You read, work on your laptop, nap, whatever. No traffic. No stress. No searching for a $30 parking spot in downtown Seattle.

I think the Sounder is genuinely underused. People hear "train" and assume it's inconvenient, but if you live near a station (Puyallup, Sumner, or Tacoma Dome especially), it can be faster door-to-door than driving during rush hour. And you get that hour back to actually do something instead of staring at brake lights.

### Driving: I-5 Corridor

I-5 is the main north-south highway connecting Pierce County to Seattle. It works. It also has traffic. Let's be honest about both.

**Peak hours** (roughly 6:30 to 9:00 AM northbound, 3:30 to 7:00 PM southbound) can be rough, especially through the Federal Way to Tacoma stretch and again through JBLM. Off-peak, the drive is totally reasonable.

**SR-167** connects the Puyallup and Bonney Lake area to the I-5/I-405 corridor through the Green River Valley. It's the main route for anyone east of I-5, and it has HOT lanes (express toll lanes) that can save time during rush hour.

**SR-512** connects Lakewood and South Tacoma to I-5 and SR-167. If you're coming from the Lakewood or Spanaway area, this is your on-ramp to everything.

The reality of driving: it's fine most of the time, and frustrating some of the time. Just like every metro area in the Pacific Northwest.

### Pierce Transit (Local Bus)

Pierce Transit runs local bus routes throughout Pierce County. It's great for getting around Tacoma, Lakewood, Puyallup, and other towns within the county. For commuting to Seattle, you'd typically combine Pierce Transit with the Sounder or Sound Transit Express buses.

### Sound Transit Express Buses

Sound Transit runs express bus routes that connect Pierce County to Seattle, Bellevue, and other regional destinations. These are a good option if the Sounder schedule doesn't line up with yours, or if you need to get somewhere the train doesn't go.

## How Long Is the Commute from Different Pierce County Areas?

This is the part everyone actually wants to know. Here are realistic commute time estimates. I'm giving you ranges because traffic exists and pretending it doesn't would be dishonest.

### Bonney Lake
- **To Tacoma:** 25 to 30 minutes
- **To Seattle:** 50 to 70 minutes (depending on traffic and route)
- **To JBLM:** 25 to 30 minutes

### Puyallup
- **To Tacoma:** About 20 minutes
- **To Seattle:** 45 to 60 minutes driving, or about 1 hour on the Sounder from Puyallup Station
- **To JBLM:** 20 to 25 minutes

### North Tacoma
- **To Seattle:** 35 to 50 minutes driving, about 1 hour on the Sounder from Tacoma Dome
- **To JBLM:** 15 to 20 minutes

### Eatonville
- **To Tacoma:** About 30 minutes
- **To Seattle:** 60 to 75 minutes
- **To JBLM:** 20 to 25 minutes

### General JBLM Access
- **From most Pierce County locations:** 15 to 30 minutes depending on where you are. Pierce County's central location relative to Joint Base Lewis-McChord is one of the reasons it's popular with military and civilian DOD employees.

## Is It Worth Driving vs. Taking the Sounder?

Let's do some quick math. This is approximate, but it tells the story.

**Driving to Seattle daily (round trip from Puyallup):**
- Gas: roughly $12 to $18 per day (depending on your vehicle)
- Parking in downtown Seattle: $15 to $30+ per day
- Wear and tear on your car
- Your sanity during I-5 traffic
- **Monthly estimate: $600 to $1,000+**

**Sounder train (round trip from Puyallup):**
- Monthly pass: roughly $100 to $150
- Park and ride: free at most stations
- You get to read, work, or sleep on the train
- **Monthly estimate: $100 to $150**

The math is pretty clear. If you're commuting to Seattle more than two or three days a week, the Sounder saves real money. And it saves something money can't buy: the stress of sitting in traffic.

## Where Are the Park and Ride Locations?

If you're taking the Sounder or an express bus, you'll want to know where to park. The major park and ride lots in Pierce County include:

- **Tacoma Dome Station** (Tacoma): Large lot, Sounder and express bus connections
- **Puyallup Station** (Puyallup): Sounder stop with parking
- **Sumner Station** (Sumner): Sounder stop with parking
- **South Tacoma Station** (Tacoma): Sounder stop
- **Lakewood Station** (Lakewood): Southern terminus of the Sounder line
- **Bonney Lake Park and Ride** (Bonney Lake): Bus connections to Sounder stations

Most of these lots fill up early on weekday mornings, so plan accordingly. If you can get there by 7:00 AM, you're usually fine. After that, it gets competitive.

## What About Remote Workers?

Okay, this is where Pierce County really shines. If you work remotely most of the time and only commute to Seattle or Tacoma one or two days a week, Pierce County gives you something incredible: space, quiet, and a lower cost of living, with the city still accessible when you need it.

I work with a lot of buyers who fit this exact profile. They left Seattle or the Eastside during the remote work shift, and now they're looking for a home with a real office, a yard, and room to breathe. Pierce County checks all those boxes. And on the days they do need to go into the office, the Sounder or a reverse-commute drive makes it totally doable.

The hybrid work schedule has honestly been one of the best things to happen to Pierce County's housing market. People are discovering what locals already know: you get more home for your money here, and you don't have to give up access to the metro area to get it.

## Which Pierce County Neighborhoods Work Best for Different Commute Patterns?

Here's my take, based on 15+ years of helping people find homes in this area:

**Daily commute to downtown Seattle:** North Tacoma or anywhere near a Sounder station (Puyallup, Sumner, Lakewood). The train makes this sustainable long-term. Driving daily will wear you down.

**Daily commute to Tacoma:** Almost anywhere in Pierce County works. Bonney Lake, Puyallup, Lakewood, Spanaway, Graham. Tacoma is centrally located and accessible from all directions.

**Hybrid (1 to 2 days/week to Seattle):** Bonney Lake, Puyallup, Orting, Eatonville, Graham. You can handle the drive once or twice a week, and you'll love coming home to the extra space.

**JBLM commute:** Lakewood, Spanaway, Puyallup, Graham, Eatonville. All within 15 to 30 minutes of the base, depending on which gate you use.

**Fully remote:** Wherever your heart takes you. Eatonville if you want mountain views. Gig Harbor if you want the water. Bonney Lake if you want the lake. You're free. Enjoy it.
