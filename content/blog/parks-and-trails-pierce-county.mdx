# Best Parks and Trails in Pierce County for Outdoor Lovers and Dog Owners

If you're the type who measures a neighborhood by how close it is to a good trail (or an off-leash dog park), Pierce County is going to make you very happy. I've lived and worked here for over 15 years, and I'm still discovering new spots. Between the old-growth forests, waterfall hikes, 22 miles of paved trail, and more off-leash dog areas than I can count, there's a lot of ground to cover.

So let's cover it.

## What Makes Pierce County So Good for Outdoor Lovers?

Honestly, the variety. You can start your morning on a beach, hit a forested canyon by lunch, and finish on a paved rail-trail before dinner. And you don't have to drive an hour between any of them. The county stretches from Puget Sound to the foothills of Mount Rainier, so you get everything from saltwater shoreline to mountain creek canyons.

Also, most of these parks are free. That's always nice.

## Where Are the Best Parks in Pierce County?

### Point Defiance Park (Tacoma)

This is the big one. Over 700 acres, 10+ miles of trails, old-growth forest, botanical gardens, a zoo and aquarium, Owen Beach, and Five Mile Drive (which is exactly what it sounds like: a five-mile loop drive through the park). I think Point Defiance is one of the most underrated urban parks in the entire Pacific Northwest. Dogs are allowed on leash on most trails, and the waterfront paths are absolutely gorgeous in every season.

If you only visit one park on this list, make it this one.

### Swan Creek Park (Tacoma)

Over 400 acres of forested canyon with mountain biking trails, hiking paths, and (this is the good part) a dedicated off-leash dog park. It's one of those places that feels way more remote than it actually is. You're technically still in Tacoma, but it doesn't feel like it once you're on the trail.

### Allan Yorke Park (Bonney Lake)

Sandy beach on Lake Tapps, boat launch, playgrounds, sports courts, a skatepark, BBQ grills, and covered picnic shelters. Allan Yorke is basically Bonney Lake's living room during the summer. It's where everyone ends up on a warm Saturday. The lake access alone makes it worth the visit, but the whole park is really well maintained.

### Victor Falls Park (Bonney Lake)

This one's a hidden gem. A forested canyon with a waterfall and a swimming hole at the bottom. The hike down is short but steep, so wear real shoes (not flip-flops; I learned that lesson). It's a totally different vibe from Allan Yorke, even though they're both in Bonney Lake. More rugged, more quiet, more "wait, this is here?"

### Chambers Bay (University Place)

You might know Chambers Bay as the site of the 2015 US Open. What you might not know is that the walking and running trail around the course has some of the best Puget Sound views in the entire county. It's paved, it's scenic, and it's a solid workout with the elevation changes. I've taken clients here after showings just to prove how close the water views are.

### Bradley Lake Park (Puyallup)

Lake, fishing, trails, playground. Bradley Lake is one of those parks that does a lot without making a big deal about it. It's a great spot if you want a mellow afternoon near the water. The loop trail around the lake is flat and easy, which makes it super accessible.

### Dash Point State Park (Federal Way/Tacoma)

Beach access, camping, and 11+ miles of trails through dense forest. Dash Point sits right on the border of Federal Way and Tacoma, and it's one of the few spots in the area where you can camp on the water. The trails range from easy to moderate, and the beach is worth the trip on its own.

### Fort Steilacoom Park (Lakewood)

One of the largest urban parks in Washington state. Fort Steilacoom has an off-leash dog area, Waughop Lake, sports fields, and miles of open space. It's flat, wide, and perfect for long walks. The off-leash area is fenced and popular, so your dog will absolutely make friends.

### Sehmel Homestead Park (Gig Harbor)

If you're on the Gig Harbor side, Sehmel Homestead has a great off-leash dog park, sports fields, and a playground. The dog park is well maintained and has separate areas for large and small dogs. It's one of those parks that clearly gets a lot of love from the community.

## What About the Foothills Trail?

The Foothills Trail deserves its own section because it's not just a park; it's a 22+ mile paved trail connecting Puyallup to Buckley through Orting and South Prairie. It follows a former railroad grade, so it's flat and smooth. Bikes, walking, running, strollers, you name it.

I think this trail is one of Pierce County's best-kept secrets, even though it's been here for years. The stretch through the Orting Valley is beautiful, with views of Mount Rainier on clear days. You can do the whole thing as a long ride or just pick a section. There are trailheads with parking in Puyallup, Orting, South Prairie, and Buckley.

Pro tip: the Orting to South Prairie section is my favorite. Less traffic, more scenery.

## Which Parks Have Off-Leash Dog Areas?

If your dog is your primary hiking partner (no judgment; mine is too), here's what you need to know:

- **Swan Creek Park (Tacoma):** Dedicated off-leash area within the 400+ acre park. Room to roam.
- **Fort Steilacoom Park (Lakewood):** Fenced off-leash area near Waughop Lake. Popular and well maintained.
- **Sehmel Homestead Park (Gig Harbor):** Separate large and small dog areas. Fenced and clean.
- **Rogers Off-Leash Dog Park (Puyallup):** A go-to for Puyallup dog owners. Fenced, with water access.

For all other parks, dogs are welcome on leash. Pierce County is pretty dog-friendly overall, but always check posted rules at trailheads. And please, bring bags. Nobody wants to step in that on a trail.

## What Are Becca's Personal Favorites?

Okay, you asked (or I'm telling you anyway).

**For a real hike:** Victor Falls. Every single time. That waterfall never gets old, and the swimming hole on a hot day is perfection.

**For a long walk with my dog:** Swan Creek. The trails are varied enough to keep it interesting, and the off-leash area means we both get to burn some energy.

**For a lazy afternoon:** Allan Yorke Park in the summer. Sandy beach, lake water, and usually someone grilling something that smells amazing nearby.

**For impressing out-of-town visitors:** Point Defiance. The scale of it genuinely surprises people. They don't expect a 700-acre old-growth forest inside city limits.

**For a bike ride or long run:** Foothills Trail, Orting to South Prairie section. Flat, scenic, and long enough to feel like you actually did something.
