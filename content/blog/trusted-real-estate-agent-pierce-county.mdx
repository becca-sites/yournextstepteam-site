There's a stat that gets thrown around in real estate: most agents close fewer than five transactions a year. Five. Total.

So when I tell you that I've worked with one couple on more than 30 deals, I want you to sit with that number for a second. Thirty. With the same two people.

Not because they're flipping houses for fun (though some of those deals were investment properties). Not because I'm the cheapest option in Pierce County. And definitely not because I bombard them with postcards every quarter.

They keep coming back because they trust me. And honestly, that's the whole story. But since you're here, probably searching for a trusted real estate agent in Pierce County, let me tell you what that trust actually looks like from the inside. Because it's not what most people expect.

## How Does a 30-Transaction Relationship Even Start?

{/* PHOTO: Candid shot of Becca reviewing documents at a kitchen table or coffee shop, natural lighting, not looking at camera */}

It started the way most of my best client relationships start: somebody knew somebody.

This couple, I'll call them M and S, came to me through a referral years ago. Their first deal was straightforward. A single-family home in Pierce County, nothing flashy, nothing complicated. They needed an agent who knew the area, understood their goals, and could get the job done without a bunch of drama.

So I did that. I showed up, did my homework on the neighborhood, negotiated a solid deal, and made sure they felt informed at every step. Not overwhelmed. Not talked down to. Just... informed.

And then they called me again.

And again.

And then they started sending their friends my way.

Here's the thing people don't realize about referrals: you can't manufacture them. You can ask for them (and I do, because I'm not shy about it). But a referral only happens when someone trusts you enough to put their own reputation on the line by recommending you. That's a big deal. M and S have referred more people to me than I can count, and every single one of those referrals is a reflection of the relationship I've built with them.

## What Makes Someone Come Back to the Same Agent 30+ Times?

I think about this question a lot, actually. Because in a world where you can Google "Pierce County real estate agent" and get 400 options in half a second, loyalty is not a given. Nobody has to come back to me. Every single transaction, they're choosing me again.

So what keeps them coming back?

**I know their story.** Not just their address history. I know what they're trying to build. I know which neighborhoods they love and which ones they've outgrown. I know their risk tolerance, their timeline preferences, their deal-breakers. When M and S call me about a potential investment property, I don't need a 45-minute intake meeting. I already know what they're looking for, what they'll walk away from, and what kind of offer strategy makes sense for their portfolio.

That kind of context takes years to build. And it's worth its weight in gold for the client.

**I tell them the truth.** This is a big one. I've talked M and S out of deals. More than once. I've said, "I don't think this is the right property for what you're trying to do," and I've meant it, even when it meant I didn't get a commission that month. Short-term, that's a hit. Long-term, it's the reason they trust me enough to call me 30-plus times.

A lot of agents won't do that. I get it. When your income depends on closings, it's tempting to say yes to everything. But I'd rather lose a deal today and keep a client for 15 years than close something that doesn't serve them.

**I make it easy.** After 30-plus transactions, M and S and I have a rhythm. They know how I communicate (text for quick stuff, email for documents, phone calls for anything complicated). I know how they make decisions (they're thorough, they want data, and they don't like being rushed). I've adapted my process to fit them, and they've learned to trust my process in return.

That kind of ease doesn't happen on transaction one. It happens over time. And it's honestly one of the most rewarding parts of this job.

## Why Does Having a Trusted Agent in Pierce County Actually Matter?

Okay, let me put on my "real estate nerd" hat for a second. Because this isn't just a feel-good story about nice clients. There are real, tangible benefits to working with an agent who knows you and knows the market deeply.

### Your Agent Remembers What the Market Did Last Time

I've been a licensed broker in Washington state for over 15 years. I've seen Pierce County go through multiple market cycles. I remember what happened during the 2008 correction. I watched the pandemic market go completely sideways in 2020 and 2021. I know which neighborhoods bounce back fast and which ones take longer. I know which builders cut corners and which ones don't.

When M and S ask me about a property in a specific part of Tacoma or Bonney Lake or Spanaway, I'm not just pulling comps from the MLS. I'm drawing on 15-plus years of watching this market breathe. I know the neighborhood history. I know why certain streets sell for more than others, even when the square footage is identical. I know which developments are about to get new infrastructure and which ones have been promised improvements for a decade with nothing to show for it.

That kind of knowledge is not something you can get from a Zillow listing. It comes from being here, doing the work, year after year.

### Your Agent Knows Your Investment Goals (Not Just Your Address)

{/* PHOTO: Aerial or elevated view of a Pierce County residential area, showing the mix of established neighborhoods and newer development, natural light */}

For clients like M and S, who have built a real estate portfolio over time, having an agent who understands their big-picture strategy is absolutely critical. I don't treat each transaction as a standalone event. I see it as one piece of a larger plan.

That means I'm thinking about things like: How does this property fit with the others they already own? What's the tax implication of buying versus selling this year? Is the rental market strong enough in this area to support their investment thesis? Are they approaching a point where a 1031 exchange makes sense?

I'm not a CPA or a financial advisor, and I always tell my clients to loop in their professionals for those conversations. But I can flag the questions they should be asking, because I've been alongside them long enough to see the full picture.

### Your Agent Advocates for You Like It's Personal

After 30-plus transactions, it kind of is personal. When M and S are in a negotiation, I'm not thinking about my commission. I'm thinking about what's right for them. I've gone to bat on inspections, pushed back on appraisals, and negotiated repairs that other agents might have let slide. Because these aren't just clients to me. They're people I've walked alongside for years, and I take that seriously.

## How Are Referrals Different From Cold Leads?

Here's something I want every person reading this to understand, because it totally changes how you think about choosing a real estate agent.

When someone refers you to their agent, they're telling you something important. They're saying, "This person did right by me, and I trust them enough to recommend them to you." That carries weight. Way more weight than a Google ad or a bus bench photo.

M and S have sent me referrals consistently for years. Not because I asked them to fill out a review card. Because their friends would mention they were thinking about buying or selling, and M and S would say, "Call Becca."

That's it. That's the whole referral strategy. Be good at your job, treat people well, stay in touch, and let the relationships do the work.

I'm a solo agent. I don't have a team of 15 people. I don't have a marketing department. My business is built on relationships, repeat clients, and referrals, and I prefer it that way. It means every client gets me. Not an assistant, not a junior agent, not a "team member" you've never met. Me.

## What Should You Look For in a Trusted Real Estate Agent in Pierce County?

If you're reading this because you're actively looking for an agent, here's my honest advice. And yes, I'm going to be a little self-serving here, but I also genuinely believe these things matter.

**Look for experience in your specific area.** Pierce County is big. Tacoma is different from Bonney Lake is different from Graham is different from Spanaway. An agent who knows your specific neighborhood, who has closed deals on your street, who understands the micro-market trends in your zip code: that agent is going to serve you better than someone who works all over the state and happens to have a listing nearby.

I focus on Pierce County. It's where I live, it's where I work, and it's where I've built my expertise over 15-plus years. When I say I know this market, I mean I really know it.

**Look for someone who communicates the way you need.** Some agents are great on the phone but terrible at responding to texts. Some are all email, all the time. The best agent for you is someone whose communication style matches yours. Ask about it upfront. It matters more than you think, especially when things get stressful mid-transaction.

**Look for longevity, not just reviews.** Five-star reviews are great. I love getting them. But also look for how long an agent has been doing this. Have they weathered a down market? Have they handled complicated deals? Have they been around long enough to have repeat clients? A newer agent with 50 five-star reviews from their first two years is not the same as an agent with 15 years of experience and clients who keep coming back.

**Look for honesty over salesmanship.** If an agent tells you everything you want to hear during the listing presentation, be cautious. A good agent will tell you the truth, even when it's not what you want to hear. They'll give you a realistic price range, a realistic timeline, and a realistic picture of what to expect. That's not pessimism. That's respect.

## What Does Trust Actually Look Like in Real Estate?

{/* PHOTO: Close-up of house keys on a countertop or being handed over, warm tones, shallow depth of field */}

Trust looks like a text at 9 PM that says, "Hey, a property just hit the market that I think fits what you're looking for. Want me to set up a showing tomorrow?"

Trust looks like an agent who calls you before you call them when something changes in a transaction.

Trust looks like hearing, "I don't think you should do this deal," and knowing that advice is coming from someone who cares more about your outcome than their paycheck.

Trust looks like 30-plus transactions with the same couple, spanning years, spanning market cycles, spanning different goals and different properties and different challenges. And still picking up the phone every single time with the same energy and the same commitment.

That's what I've built with M and S. And it's what I try to build with every single client who calls me. Not every relationship will turn into 30 deals. Most won't. But every relationship gets the same level of care, the same honesty, and the same commitment.

Because at the end of the day, that's what being a trusted real estate agent in Pierce County actually means. It's not about the marketing. It's not about the fancy headshots or the clever taglines. It's about showing up, doing the work, telling the truth, and being someone worth calling again.
