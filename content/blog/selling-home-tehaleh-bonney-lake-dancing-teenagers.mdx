**The details**
Columbia Vista at Cascadia, Tehaleh, [Bonney Lake](/areas/bonney-lake) (Pierce County) | Single family, 4 bed, 2.5 bath, about 2,790 square feet, built 2014 | I represented the seller | Originally listed at $715,000, reduced to $699,999, sold at $690,000 | Closed summer 2026 | 40 days on market | For context: my other Bonney Lake listings have gone in a median of 5 days

> **The takeaway:** Creative marketing is not a substitute for a market that isn't there. It's what you do while you wait for the right buyer, so that when they show up, they've heard of you.

I'm going to start with the part that didn't work.

I listed at $715,000. I cut to $699,999. It sold for $690,000, forty days in. That's twenty-five thousand dollars below where I started, and forty days is the longest a Bonney Lake listing of mine has ever sat. The median for my Bonney Lake homes is five days. Not five weeks. Five days.

So no, this one didn't go the way I wanted. Let me tell you what I did about it, including the part where I hired teenagers.

## The market wasn't cooperating, and neither was the HOA

That summer, Tehaleh was saturated. Very few sales happening at all, which meant buyers had options and no reason to hurry.

Then there was the sign problem.

Tehaleh doesn't allow A-board signs in the neighborhood. Plenty of communities have a version of that rule, but this one was being enforced. Put a sign out, and somebody came along and picked it up.

I get why the rule exists. Nobody wants their streets turning into a forest of plastic every Saturday. But here's what it does to a seller: Tehaleh is big, it's master-planned, and it's built in sub-neighborhoods. Ours was Columbia Vista at Cascadia. You don't stumble onto a house out here. Somebody has to tell you where to turn.

So in the slowest market I'd seen in Bonney Lake, I also couldn't point anybody toward the front door.

## So I hired teenagers

You can pull a sign out of the ground. You can't confiscate a kid holding one.

That was the idea. I hired teenagers, put them in ice cream parlor outfits, gave them signs that said "Open House with Ice Cream," and had them dance on the corner.

I placed them in two spots on purpose. One group at the entrance roundabout, where everybody coming into Tehaleh has to slow down anyway. The second group at the main entrance to our sub-neighborhood. Catch them coming in, hand them off at the turn.

When I pitched it, my seller said it was fun and very me. She wasn't wrong. And honestly, there was a little bit of stick-it-to-the-man in it. Not because I have anything against her HOA, but because a rule that makes it harder for one of their own homeowners to sell deserves a creative answer.

## Here's the part I got wrong

I thought the teenagers would fill the open house. They didn't, really. A few more people than a normal weekend, but not the flood I was picturing.

What actually happened is that Tehaleh started talking about it.

The community Facebook group lit up. People posting about the dancing kids, asking which house it was, debating whether it was even allowed, sharing it around. There was more conversation happening in that group about my marketing than there was foot traffic at the door.

That turned out to be worth more. In a master-planned community, the neighbors aren't bystanders. They're the distribution channel. They're the ones with a sister who's been trying to get into Tehaleh for a year, or a coworker who keeps asking what it's like out here.

But I want to be honest about the limits of that, because this is where a lot of agent marketing stories quietly stop telling the truth. The Facebook buzz helped. It did not save us from a price reduction. We still came down twice, and it still sold for less than I asked. Creative marketing is not a substitute for a market that isn't there.

## The sale nobody saw from the outside

Here's what I haven't told you yet.

My seller bought that house with money she inherited from her father. And in the middle of marketing it, before there was a signed deal, her mother died.

So she was selling a home her dad's money bought, while burying her mom, while getting ready to start over in Arizona. For the first time in her life, she was making a decision this size without a parent to call.

We didn't pause the listing. I took the reins a little bit harder.

I don't think there's anything heroic in that. You just don't hand somebody a decision tree on the worst week of their year. You keep moving the parts you can move, you bring her the things that genuinely need her, and you handle the rest. That's the whole job in a month like that.

Now go back and look at those forty days. Forty days of a saturated market and two price reductions, in the middle of the hardest thing that had ever happened to her. That's why I was out on a roundabout hiring dancers. Sitting still was not a plan I was willing to offer her.

## Then the contract had a mistake in it

Because of course it did.

When the offer finally came in, the buyer's agent had forgotten to ask for their commission. It just wasn't in the contract.

That's a real problem, and it's been happening more since the rules around buyer agent compensation changed. Everybody's still adjusting. Miss that line, and an agent does months of work for free.

I could have stayed quiet. It wasn't my mistake and it wasn't my client's problem.

But I've been licensed since 2010, and the agent across the table today is the one reading my client's offer next spring. More to the point, I don't think you get to call yourself good at this if the way you win is hoping the other person doesn't catch their own error.

So we sorted it out. Everybody gave in a bit, nobody blew up the deal, and we found the win-win. That's usually available if somebody goes looking for it.

It closed at $690,000.

## What I did that another agent wouldn't have

1. **I built the marketing around the HOA rule instead of around a sign.** The covenant bans placed signs, not people. Sign spinners were a legal answer to a real constraint.
2. **I told my seller the truth about the price twice.** Two reductions in forty days is not a fun conversation. It's the honest one.
3. **I carried the transaction while she was grieving,** and brought her only the decisions that actually needed her.
4. **I flagged the other agent's missing commission instead of letting it ride.** That cost my side something in the negotiation and it was still the right call.
5. **I'm telling you the marketing didn't do what I expected.** The open house traffic barely moved. The community conversation is what mattered. You should know which one worked.

## What it actually takes to sell in Tehaleh

If you're thinking about selling out here, some things are true that aren't true elsewhere in Bonney Lake.

Tehaleh is a master-planned community off SR 410, platted originally as Cascadia, built out in named sub-neighborhoods like Columbia Vista. Buyers come for the trail system and the way the neighborhoods are laid out, and most of them have picked the community before they ever call an agent. They're choosing between a handful of similar homes in one pocket, not between Tehaleh and the rest of Pierce County.

The HOA rules are real and they get enforced. Build your marketing around them instead of hoping nobody notices.

And price honestly from the start. I started at $715,000 in a summer that wouldn't support it, and the market took its twenty-five thousand back over forty days. I'd rather tell you that than pretend the ice cream fixed everything.

## After closing

She moved to Arizona. I helped her get out of the house, went back to the new owners to pick up a few things she'd left behind, coordinated her storage, and programmed her computer mouse, which is not a real estate service but was apparently needed. My fee for that was one chocolate.

In September she went under contract on a home down there, through an agent I referred her to.

## Questions I get about selling in Tehaleh

**Can you hold an open house in Tehaleh if the HOA bans signs?**
Yes. The rule covers placed A-board signs, not people. Sign spinners, the community Facebook group, and directional help at the entrance roundabout all work, and none of it violates the covenant.

**How long does it take to sell a home in Tehaleh?**
It swings hard with the season and inventory. This one took 40 days in a saturated summer with very few comparable sales. My Bonney Lake listings have gone in a median of 5 days, so 40 was an outlier and the market was the reason.

**Will I have to reduce my price in Tehaleh?**
You might. This one started at $715,000 and sold at $690,000. In a quiet stretch with few comps, the first price is a hypothesis, not a promise.

**What do Tehaleh buyers actually care about?**
Most have already chosen the community. They're picking a sub-neighborhood and a floor plan, usually for the trails and the layout. Your competition is the few similar homes in your pocket, not all of Bonney Lake.

**Does marketing in the community Facebook group actually work?**
In a master-planned community, better than almost anything else. Your neighbors know people who want to live here. Give them something worth talking about.

**Can I sell a home while I'm dealing with a death in the family?**
People do it, and it's survivable. What matters is working with somebody who will carry the logistics so you can spend your attention where it actually belongs.
