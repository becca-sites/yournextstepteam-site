## Why pre-list prep matters more than any single marketing decision

A finished, photographed, well-staged home is the asset. Everything that happens after listing day is a multiplier on top of that asset. If the asset is rough, every multiplier underperforms. If the asset is polished, every multiplier compounds.

## Week six: the walkthrough

The first step is a real walkthrough with a fresh set of eyes. We look at the home the way a first-time buyer will look at it on a Saturday morning. Light, flow, smell, the front door approach, the path from car to kitchen, the first ten feet of every room.

## Weeks five and four: the prioritized punch list

A contractor walks the home with us and gives a prioritized list. Must-do, nice-to-have, skip. The goal is not to remodel. The goal is to remove the small friction points that quietly cost real dollars when buyers walk through.

## Weeks three and two: staging and refresh

This is where the styling work happens. Furniture placement, art, soft goods, light fixtures, fresh paint where it matters most. The point is not to make the home look bigger than it is. The point is to make the home photograph the way it actually feels at its best.

## Week one: photo, video, copy

A photographer for stills. A drone operator for aerials when the lot or the view earns it. A cinematic walkthrough video, even at the modest end of the scale. And listing copy that says what the home actually is, instead of generic real estate filler.

## Listing day

By the time the sign goes in, the home is ready. The buyers showing up on Saturday morning are seeing a finished product. That is what makes the difference between an offer in 48 hours and a price reduction in 30 days.
