<p className="speakable">
The Snoqualmie Valley closed late spring with median sale prices holding firm in the mid-$800s, days on market drifting up slightly, and inventory finally giving buyers a bit more room to breathe.
</p>

## What the numbers say

Late spring brought a small but real shift. Median sale prices across the primary valley markets settled in around $850,000 for Duvall, $820,000 for Carnation, and $950,000 for Snoqualmie. Compared to the same window last year, that's roughly flat for Duvall and Carnation and up modestly in Snoqualmie.

Inventory finished the month higher than it has been since fall. Active listings climbed about 18 percent month over month. Days on market drifted from 12 days to 19. Both moves are healthy for buyers, and they don't yet signal a price correction.

## What is actually moving

Three patterns stood out this month.

The first is the return of move-up families. After a year of buyers parking and waiting, we're seeing more sell-and-buy pairs come back, especially in the Snoqualmie ridge neighborhoods and out to Carnation acreage. Coordinating a contingent sale and a contingent purchase is a craft, and the families who are nailing it this spring all have one thing in common: they started prep work three months before listing.

The second is price discipline. Listings priced right are still selling in single digits of days. Listings priced 5 to 10 percent over comp data are sitting on market for 30 to 45 days and then taking price reductions. The market has not lost its sensitivity to overpricing.

The third is the inventory build in the $1.2M to $1.5M range. There is more competition in that band than there was 90 days ago, and homes in that range are now negotiating from a different posture than the homes under $900,000.

## What it means for sellers

If you are thinking about listing in the next 90 days, the pre-list window matters more than usual. Walk-through, light prep, a clean staging plan, and a thoughtful price all multiply right now.

## What it means for buyers

You have a little more room than you did in February. Not a lot, but enough that strong inspections and clean contract terms are getting accepted more often than the high-stress escalation clauses we were writing all winter.

## What to watch next month

Rate moves are still the wild card. A 25 basis-point shift in either direction will reshape buyer demand fast. We will keep watching active inventory, pending counts, and the gap between list price and sale price.
