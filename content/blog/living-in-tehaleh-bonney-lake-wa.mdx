So, I get asked this a lot: "Becca, do you actually live in Tehaleh, or do you just sell houses here?" Both. I live here. I walk these trails every morning before I open my laptop, and most days I still stop halfway through my walk and just look around like it's my first time seeing it.

I don't just sell homes in Tehaleh. I live here. That matters, because when a buyer asks me what it's actually like on a random Tuesday night, or whether the HOA fee is worth it, or whether the commute is as bad as people say, I'm not guessing. I'm telling you what my own life looks like.

I moved here because I wanted trails I could walk to instead of drive to, and I wanted to be part of a community that felt intentional rather than just a subdivision with a nice sign out front. Tehaleh delivered on both. It's not perfect (I'll get into that honestly later), but it's home, and I think that gives me a perspective most guides to this neighborhood just don't have.

## What Is Tehaleh, and Why Is Everyone Talking About It?

Tehaleh is a master-planned community in Bonney Lake, Washington, sitting in the foothills of the Cascades with Mt. Rainier visible from a lot of vantage points throughout the neighborhood. It's big. Like, really big. The community spans about 4,700 acres, which is one of the larger master-planned developments in the Pacific Northwest.

Newland Communities is the developer behind Tehaleh, and the community opened its first neighborhoods back in 2012. It's been building out steadily ever since, and at full buildout, Tehaleh is planned to include up to 9,700 homes. That's essentially a small city's worth of housing, built in phases over what will end up being a couple of decades.

Here's the part that I think actually sets Tehaleh apart from a typical new construction development: over 1,800 acres are permanently preserved as open space. That's not a marketing number. That's nearly 40% of the entire community set aside and protected, forest, meadow, wetland, all of it woven between the neighborhoods instead of pushed to the edges.

Within Bonney Lake, Tehaleh functions almost like its own little world. It has its own amenities, its own trail network, its own gathering spaces. But it's still part of Bonney Lake for city services, addresses, and governance. So when people say "everyone's talking about Tehaleh," I think it's because it scratches an itch a lot of Pierce County buyers have: newer construction, real outdoor access, and a sense of community that doesn't feel manufactured.

## What Amenities Does Tehaleh Actually Have?

Okay, this is the fun part to write about because I actually use most of this stuff weekly.

The centerpiece is **The Post**. It's the main community center and the primary trailhead for the whole trail system. Inside, there's **Post and Pour**, the community coffee shop and taphouse (my go-to before showings, I'm not going to lie), gas fireplaces that make it feel cozy even in the gray months, and an outdoor patio that's genuinely one of my favorite places to sit with a coffee and watch the neighborhood go by.

There's also **The Great Room**, another gathering space for community events and programming, and **The Meadow**, which is exactly what it sounds like: an open green space that hosts events and just gives people room to spread out.

For families with younger kids and for anyone who just likes being outside, Tehaleh has 17 parks scattered throughout the community. Some are small pocket parks, some are bigger with play structures and sport courts. There's also **Discovery Park Community Center**, which adds another dedicated gathering and recreation space.

A few specific spots I want to call out because they surprised me when I first moved in:

**The Edge** is a skate park with a graffiti wall (an actual sanctioned one, so nobody's getting in trouble) and a half basketball court. It's a fun, kind of unexpected amenity for a community this size.

**Hounds Hollow** is the off-leash dog park, and if you have a dog, you will end up here. I don't have one myself, but half my neighbors do, and this is basically the social hub for the dog-owning crowd in Tehaleh.

**The Trek** is over 6 miles of dedicated mountain bike trail, built specifically for riders rather than just repurposed walking paths. I'm not a mountain biker, but I hear from neighbors who are that it's a legitimately good trail system, not just a token gesture.

Tehaleh also runs regular community events throughout the year, seasonal gatherings, fitness classes, that kind of thing. My honest personal favorites are Post and Pour on a slow weekday morning and the patio at The Meadow when there's an event going on. It's the closest thing I've found to a small-town feel inside a modern master-planned community.

## What Do the Trails Look Like in Tehaleh?

This is honestly the number one reason I live here.

Tehaleh has more than 40 miles of trails winding through the community's preserved forests and meadows. They're not an afterthought squeezed between houses. They're the backbone of how the neighborhoods connect to each other and to the amenities.

On a clear day, and we get more of those out here than people expect, you catch views of Mt. Rainier from several points along the trail system. I've genuinely stopped mid-walk more times than I can count just to stare at it. It doesn't get old, and I say that as someone who's walked these same paths probably a thousand times at this point.

The Post functions as the main trailhead, so most of my walks start and end there, usually with a coffee bookending the trip. I walk these trails every morning, rain or shine (mostly rain, this is the Pacific Northwest), and it's become the thing I recommend to every single buyer who's on the fence about Tehaleh. Come walk the trails with me, or without me, and you'll understand the appeal in about ten minutes.

For mountain bikers, The Trek adds over 6 miles of purpose-built trail on top of the general walking and hiking network, so it's not just a pedestrian amenity. Between the two systems, there's a genuinely rare amount of dedicated outdoor infrastructure for a community this size.

## Should You Buy New Construction or Resale in Tehaleh?

I get this question constantly, so let's break it down honestly.

### New Construction

Tehaleh has an unusually deep bench of builders active in the community, including Richmond American, Lennar, Pulte, Quadrant Homes, Shea Homes, Conner Homes, MainVue Homes, Tri Pointe Homes, and Century Communities. That's a lot of options under one roof, so to speak, and it means you can compare floor plans, price points, and included features without leaving the neighborhood.

Entry-level new construction starts around $495,000, and pricing climbs from there up to $775,000 or more depending on the builder, the floor plan, the lot, and your finish selections. The median new construction listing price in Tehaleh currently sits around $778,000.

I think new construction here is a genuinely strong option, especially for buyers who want to walk through model homes, compare builders side by side in a single afternoon, and make a decision with real information instead of guessing from a website. One thing I tell every buyer before they walk into a builder's sales office: bring your own agent. The builder's sales rep works for the builder. I work for you. That distinction matters most during negotiations on lot premiums, upgrades, and contract terms, and it doesn't cost you anything extra to have representation.

### Resale

Resale homes in Tehaleh currently range from about $622,000 up to $1.185 million, depending on age, size, lot position, and upgrades. Since Tehaleh has been building out since 2012, resale inventory now spans homes that are barely a few years old up to homes from the earlier phases, so you get real variety in what "resale" even means here.

The appeal of resale is speed and certainty. You can see exactly what you're buying, established landscaping is already in, and you're not waiting 6 to 12 months for a home to be built. For buyers who want the Tehaleh lifestyle now, resale is often the faster path to closing.

My honest personal take: I don't think there's a wrong answer here. It comes down to your timeline and how much you want to customize. If you can wait and want to pick your own finishes, new construction is worth the wait. If you want to move in this quarter and see exactly what you're getting, resale is the smarter play. I've helped buyers close both ways in this community, and both groups are happy with the choice they made.

## What Are the Schools Serving Tehaleh?

Tehaleh is served by the Sumner-Bonney Lake School District #320.

**Tehaleh Heights Elementary** is located directly within the community at 17520 Berkeley Parkway E. It serves grades K through 5 and currently enrolls approximately 627 students. Because it's physically located inside Tehaleh, it's one of the more convenient elementary options for families living in the community.

If school assignment matters to your home search, I always recommend confirming the specific attendance boundaries for any address you're considering directly with the district, since boundaries can shift and don't always follow the patterns you'd expect.

## How's the Commute From Tehaleh?

Let me give you real numbers, not marketing numbers.

To Tacoma, it's about 16 miles, roughly 25 minutes under normal conditions.

To Seattle, it's about 35 miles, and roughly 45 minutes, though that number is heavily traffic dependent. Commute hours will add time, and I'm not going to pretend otherwise.

To Joint Base Lewis-McChord, it's roughly 40 to 50 minutes, which makes Tehaleh a workable option for military-connected buyers, and there's a noticeable presence of JBLM-connected households in the community.

Here's my honest take as someone who actually makes these drives. If you're commuting into downtown Seattle five days a week, it's a real trade-off, and you should go in with clear eyes about it. But if you're hybrid, remote, commuting toward Tacoma, or stationed at JBLM, the math works out just fine. And what you get in exchange, the trails, the space, the views, honestly makes it worth it for most of the buyers I work with out here. It was worth it for me.

## What Does It Actually Cost to Live in Tehaleh?

This is the part that doesn't always make it into the glossy brochures, so let's talk numbers.

The Tehaleh Owners Association dues run about $88 a month. That covers maintenance of the community's shared trails, parks, and common amenities like The Post and The Great Room, plus landscaping of shared spaces and community programming.

On top of that, some neighborhoods within Tehaleh have their own sub-association fees, which I've seen run anywhere from about $342 to $350 a month depending on the specific section and builder. That's a meaningful number, so before you fall in love with a floor plan, I always pull the exact HOA and sub-association costs for my buyers so there are no surprises at closing.

As for the market itself, homes in Tehaleh are currently selling in about 44 days on average, with roughly 2 offers typical per listing. The median sale price sits at $759,495, up about 2.8% year over year. That tells me demand here is steady and healthy without being the kind of frantic bidding war environment some Puget Sound markets see.

## What's My Honest Take on Living in Tehaleh?

I live here, so it would be easy to just write a glowing list and call it done. But honest is kind of my whole thing, so here's the real version.

What I love: the trails, obviously. I can walk out my door and be on a trail within a couple minutes, and that's genuinely changed my daily routine for the better. I love that The Post feels like an actual gathering place and not just a marketing amenity. I love that on a clear morning, Mt. Rainier just shows up in my field of view like it's no big deal. It's a big deal. I still think it's a big deal every single time.

What I wish were different: Tehaleh is still an active development, which means there's ongoing construction in some sections. Some days that means construction traffic or the general noise of a neighborhood that's still growing. I also think the sub-association fees can catch buyers off guard if nobody walks them through the full cost picture up front, so I make a point of doing that with every single buyer I work with here.

Overall, though, I chose this community on purpose, and I'd choose it again. It's not for everyone. If a short commute to downtown Seattle is non-negotiable, or if you want the character of an older, established neighborhood with mature trees and historic homes, Tehaleh isn't going to be your fit. But if you want newer construction, real trail access, and a community that's built around getting outside, I think it's genuinely one of the best options in this part of Pierce County. I wouldn't live anywhere else right now.
